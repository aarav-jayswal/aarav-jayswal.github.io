Microstrip Directional Couplers

Keysight ADS Linecalc VNA S-Parameters

Project Details

  • Synthesized multi-stage microstrip coupled lines using the ADS Linecalc utility on an $\epsilon_{r}=4.3$, $h=1.58$ mm substrate to design precise $-10$ dB and $-20$ dB directional couplers operating at $2.0$ GHz[cite: 27].
  • Simulated the high-frequency S-parameters ($S_{11}$, $S_{21}$, $S_{31}$, $S_{41}$) across a broad $0$ to $6$ GHz spectrum to mathematically evaluate input reflection, port isolation, coupling values, and transmission coefficients[cite: 28].
  • Modeled non-ideal physical layout discontinuities by integrating custom microstrip corners and $50~\Omega$ transmission lines directly into the coupler ports[cite: 28].
  • Calibrated and operated a physical Vector Network Analyzer (VNA) from $300$ kHz to $3$ GHz to experimentally characterize both a commercial Mini-Circuits coupler and a custom-fabricated microstrip coupler[cite: 29].
  • Measured and compared empirical mid-band reflection coefficients, isolated port leakage, and usable frequency bandwidths by selectively terminating isolated ports with matched $50~\Omega$ loads[cite: 29, 30].

Simulation & Hardware Verification

Designing and characterizing these directional couplers highlighted the real-world deviations from ideal theoretical models. The ADS simulations accurately mapped how increasing the physical line spacing directly altered the even and odd mode impedances, effectively reducing the coupling factor[cite: 27]. Subsequent hardware verification using the VNA confirmed these theoretical behaviors, verifying the strict inverse relationship between direct transmission power and coupled power while maintaining tight port isolation under load[cite: 29, 30, 31].
